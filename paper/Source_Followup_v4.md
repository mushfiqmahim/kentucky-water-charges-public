The p35 table reconciles internally: 2,908,014 + 47,791 + 9,558 = 2,965,363; less 33,930 + 975 + 8,846 + 1,250,850 leaves 1,670,762; less 992,944 gives 677,818. The p37 amount is $31,667 lower. The inspected pages do not establish an exact bridge to that summary amount. Appendix B uses $1,148,640 in adjusted present wholesale revenue, while p35 deducts proposed wholesale revenue; those are different concepts, and their difference does not explain $31,667. The original staff recommendation on p3 is $698,176, also not $646,151. No claim that the summary is a typo, or that the retail and water-service figures are interchangeable, is justified here.

The main paper uses the reconciled overall requirement and approved phases, and omits both competing revenue-increase amounts as numerical findings. They are preserved here with definitions. The benchmark calculations remain $29.51 → $43.24 → $49.80; pp38, 41–42 establish phase-one service on the order date and phase two on its first anniversary. The order describes a two-year phase-in to reduce rate shock (p36). Revenue requirements are regulatory pro-forma constructs; neither required revenue nor standardized price growth is observed cost growth or receipts.

The current panel gives Knott accounts 3,055 → 3,170, +3.7643208%, and charge growth +68.7563538%. Hyden accounts 3,412 → 3,372, −1.1723329%, and charge growth +86.8170267%. This is a comparison of heterogeneous cases, not matched causal controls.

## Mountain surcharge cap

The September 2, 2026 order in case 2025-00327 contains a further internal discrepancy. The narrative on PDF p38 describes the existing $1.72 surcharge as limited to 48 months or $1,365,608 assessed. Appendix B, PDF p49, instead specifies four years from January 2024 or $1,368,516 collected, in both phase schedules. The caps differ by $2,908, and the assessed/collected wording also differs. Both duration formulations support 48 months, but neither expressly reconciles the April 2024 order’s January 2024–December 2028 dates.

These statements are preserved without choosing a corrected cap or inferring a cessation date. They create uncertainty about the surcharge’s cessation conditions and later billing history. They do not provide evidence that the supported December 2024 $1.72 component, or the $56.63 benchmark including it, should change. The event data continue to record the original authorization cap; neither amount is substituted for the other as a resolved cessation condition.
